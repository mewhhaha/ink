ink/sound

10 types · 42 values

What the game plays this frame (protocol/schema.json's buses, voices and sounds), which every host mixes alike, with one shared reverb:

  • Buses: the frame's mix, the master first, then the game's own (music, effects, ambience and interface here). Each has a gain, a lowpass and a send to the reverb; on the master the send is how loud the reverb comes back. host.blot puts the player's volumes on them.
  • Voices: loops and long sounds the game keeps playing, declared every frame they play, whole, by an id that stays the same. Hosts start a new id, fade out one the frames leave out, and glide what changes, so nothing is ever stopped or put back in step: a reload, a rewind or frames run unseen just declare what plays then.
  • Sounds: played once, from somewhere in the world (at), panned, quieted, dulled and made wetter by where it is from the camera, or from the interface (ui), in the middle.

Clips index the asset catalog's sounds; pitch 1 plays a clip as recorded. Gains are linear, pans -1 left to 1 right, lowpass cutoffs Hz (open for none) and reverb sends linear.

Key items: Bus, Listener, Panner, ReverbZone, empty_access.

func voice 
func voice: U32 -> U32 -> U32 -> Voice

A voice playing clip through bus, which id (1 to 0xFFFFFF, the same every frame) names: looping from the clip's start, at full gain, in the middle, as recorded, open and dry, fading in and out briefly. Change what differs, then keep it each frame it plays. A voice that comes back while it fades out returns where it is; one whose clip or bus changes starts again, the old fading out as the new fades in; one that does not loop plays once and is silent from then on, for as long as frames keep it.
