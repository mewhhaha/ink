data ReflectionProbe 
type ReflectionProbe is data

A local reflection probe: the host draws the scene from position as a cube of six faces when the probe's revision changes (or capture rises from false to true), a few faces a frame (Settings.reflection_budget), keeps it prefiltered under id, and glossy surfaces inside the influence box (the box of half size extent round centre) reflect it. Inside the box's faces the probe fades in over blend metres (0 for a hard edge), and where boxes overlap the higher priority takes the reflection first while equal ones share it by their weights (ink/reflections is the reference). The cube is looked up as if its surroundings were the box of half size parallax_extent round parallax_centre (box projection: a room's walls where its walls are); a half size of 0 or less on any axis looks it up as if infinitely far. far is how far the capture sees. The influence box must include the surfaces that should reflect it, floors included (a floor on a face of the box is outside it). At most 64 probes a frame; the host keeps 8 cubes.

data Fog 
type Fog is data

The participating medium: a mist beyond radius of the origin, whose extinction density per world unit falls off exponentially with height above y = 0 over height, drawn through a froxel volume out to distance ahead of the camera (lit by the sun, the lights and the sky, with shafts and shadows; ink/volumetrics is the reference) and analytically beyond it; density 0 and no fog volumes for none. edge is how far past radius the mist takes to rise from nothing to its full density, smoothly (0 for a hard edge). distance 0 draws the mist analytically alone, with no local volumes. albedo is the share of light the mist scatters, by channel, and anisotropy the Henyey-Greenstein g (-0.95 to 0.95) of its phase function: positive scatters toward where the light is going (shafts toward the sun), negative back toward it, 0 evenly. sun scales the sun's in-scattering (0 for none) and ambient the sky's and the probes'; sky (0 to 1) is how far the ambient colour is the sky's own in the direction looked rather than its mean light all round (1 fades distant objects into the sky behind them). noise (0 to 1) is how much a 3D value noise varies the global mist's density (0 uniform, 1 from nothing to twice the mean; the mean stays what it was), noise_scale the size in world units of its coarsest cell (finer octaves follow, as many as the volumetrics tier allows) and drift how far the game has carried it along x, y and z (wind times time): the same drift draws the same mist.

data Exposure 
type Exposure is data

How the camera exposes the frame. With auto off, compensation is a fixed exposure in stops (a game's brightness option belongs here; 0 leaves the light as it is). With auto on, the host meters the scene on the GPU and the exposure adapts toward the one that brings the trimmed average of the light to target (0.18, middle grey) plus compensation: the average is of the log2 luminance of the pixels between the low and high percentiles (shares of the picture, so the darkest and brightest are ignored), centre 0 to 1 weighs the middle of the picture more than its edges, and the histogram tells luminances apart from range_low to range_high stops of log2 (1 is SDR white). The exposure stays within min and max stops and moves toward the goal by 1 - exp(-rate * dt) of the distance each frame, at the brighten rate (per second) when the scene darkens and darken when it brightens, so frame rate does not change the curve. dt is the game's seconds since the last frame: the host's adapter sets it (0 while the game is paused, which holds the exposure). cut jumps to the goal this frame, for a camera cut or a teleport; the adapter also sets it for the first frame after a restore (app.invalidate), so an earlier moment is not shown with the exposure of the one it replaced. The exposure itself stays on the GPU, never read back to the game. Reset every frame by begin_frame.

data Field 
type Field is data

A patch of a field: a grid of width x height values the host keeps for material, which every draw of it reads as a texture (its shader's binding 3, red channel, half-float precision; read it with textureLoad). The patch's values, columns to a row, land row by row at column x, row z. The host keeps each value until a later patch overwrites it, so a game sends what changed, and every value again now and then for a frame it may have skipped. A field draws as zero until its values arrive; its draws carry no data of their own, so they can share an instanced draw.

Constructors

$Field
  • material: U32
