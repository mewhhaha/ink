ink/render/lighting

11 types · 42 values

Physically based GGX materials, direct lighting, clearcoat, and shading reference math.

Key items: Material, Light, Sample, Coat, product.

data Material 
type Material is data

A surface's base: base colour, metallic and perceptual roughness, the light it emissions and what sets its face-on Fresnel reflectance f0: the index of refraction ior and the KHR_materials_specular specular weight and specular_color (an ior of 1.5 and a specular of 1 with a white tint give 0.04, the glTF default).

data Coat 
type Coat is data

What a material adds to its base, each group off at weight 0 and costing nothing then. Coat: a clear coat of weight and roughness. Sheen: a cloth fuzz of tint color and roughness. Anisotropy: stretching of the highlight along the tangent frame by strength in [-1, 1], the frame turned about the normal by rotation radians. Transmission: weight of the light passing through, a slab of thickness in world units (0 is thin walled), whose attenuation_color is what remains after attenuation_distance (0 is none) of travel. Subsurface: amount of light scattered below the surface, of tint color; thin is how opaque the slab is (1 opaque, 0 lets all the back light through).

func sheen_albedo 
func sheen_albedo: F32 -> F32 -> F32

The sheen's directional albedo for a white tint, seen at cosine nv with roughness roughness (clamped to [0.07, 1]): the integral of D V nl over the hemisphere of lights, capped at 1, as a 32 x 16 table interpolated bilinearly. Columns are evenly spaced in sqrt(nv) (nv 0 to 1), rows in sqrt((roughness - 0.07) / 0.93). Row j, column i is entry 32 j + i; the table at the end of this file is generated and checked by the native reference tests.

func surface_response 
func surface_response: Surface -> Frame -> Vec3 -> Color

A surface's response to the light direction light (toward the light), per unit radiance with NoL included, as brdf is for a material: the base lobes layered with the surface's coat, sheen, anisotropy, subsurface and the non-transmitted share of its transmission. A surface with none of those is exactly brdf. shade_surface is the same response scaled by a sample's radiance. A zero vector is no direction and a back-facing view or a light below the horizon shade nothing (see layered).

func coat_response

The clear coat for one light: its own response (weight * Fc(vh) * D * V * nlc, with Smith's V at the coat normal's cosines to the light and the view, and Fc from the half vector's cosine to the view) and what the layers below keep: 1 - weight * Fc(nvc), the coat's Fresnel at the coat normal's cosine to the view, the same for every light.

func bent_normal

The normal image-based light is looked up with: the shading normal bent toward the direction the highlight is stretched, normalize(mix(n, bent, |a| clamp(5 r, 0, 1))) where the bent normal is the view's part perpendicular to the grain, the bitangent for a >= 0 and the tangent otherwise. The shading normal where the mix vanishes.

func aniso_reflection 
func aniso_reflection: Material -> Anisotropy -> Frame -> Vec3

func subsurface_response 
func subsurface_response: Material -> Subsurface -> Frame -> Vec3 -> Color

The diffuse lobe with the surface's subsurface for one light, per unit radiance: base (1 - F) (1 - metallic) / pi times the clamped cosine blended with the wrapped one (see Subsurface), plus the light that comes through, amount * color * (1 - thin) * back * base / pi * 2. A surface with no subsurface gives Lambert's lobe. nl is not clamped before the wrap, so a light behind the surface still lights it, and facing still matters.

func refract 
func refract: Vec3 -> Vec3 -> F32 -> Vec3

The refraction of the unit direction incident entering a surface of unit normal with the ratio of indices eta (incoming over outgoing), as GLSL's and WGSL's refract: eta * i - (eta (n . i) + sqrt(k)) n with k = 1 - eta^2 (1 - (n . i)^2), and the zero vector where k < 0 (total internal reflection).

func transmission_ray 
func transmission_ray: F32 -> Frame -> Vec3

The direction a ray from the eye takes through a volume of index ior: refract(-v, n, 1 / ior) for the frame's unit shading normal and view (an ior below 1 counts as 1), the zero vector at total internal reflection.

func volume_exit 
func volume_exit: Vec3 -> Vec3 -> F32 -> Vec3

Where that ray leaves a volume of thickness_world (the material's thickness times the model's x-axis scale) from point: a thin-walled surface (thickness 0) exits where it entered.

func volume_attenuation 
func volume_attenuation: Color -> F32 -> F32 -> Color

What Beer-Lambert absorption leaves of white light after thickness_world through a medium that leaves color after distance: color to the power thickness_world / distance per channel. No distance (0 or less) or no thickness is no attenuation.

func transmission_level 
func transmission_level: F32 -> F32 -> F32 -> F32 -> F32

The mip level of the opaque picture a transmissive surface of roughness and ior reads, as the glTF sample viewer picks it: `log2(width) * clamp(r

  • clamp(2 ior - 2, 0, 1), 0, 1)` with r = clamp(roughness, 0.04, 1), at

most last, the picture's last level. width is the picture's width in pixels.

func transmit 
func transmit: Surface -> Color -> Color -> F32 -> F32 -> Color

The light a transmissive surface shows from behind it: weight * T with, per channel, T = behind * base * attenuation * (1 - Fspec) * (1 - metallic), behind the opaque picture read at the ray's exit (see transmission_ray, volume_exit, transmission_level), attenuation from volume_attenuation and Fspec = f0 * split_x + split_y, the split-sum table's value at the view's cosine and the roughness. The diffuse lobe's own share is 1 - weight.

func parallax_layers 
func parallax_layers: F32 -> U32

How many layers a march takes along a direction of tangent-space cosine: floor(mix(32, 8, |cosine|)), with mix(a, b, t) = a + (b - a) t in F32, 32 straight on and 8 at grazing angles.

func parallax_uv 
func parallax_uv: (F32 -> (F32 -> F32)) -> F32 -> F32 -> Vec3 -> F32 -> (F32, F32)

The displaced coordinates: march N layers down from (u, v) at height 1, layer k at depth k / N and at (u, v) + k step with step = -view.xy / z * scale / N and z = max(view.z, 1e-4), from k = 0 until the first layer whose ray height 1 - k / N is at or below the sampled height, then one linear refinement between that sample and the one before it, where the gap (sampled height - ray height) changes sign. A view that is not above the surface (z <= 0) or a zero vector does not displace.

const parallax_shadow_gain 
const parallax_shadow_gain: F32

How much of the light reaches the surface point at (u, v), 0 (shadowed) to 1: from the point at its height h, march toward the light, which rises 1 - h over N = parallax_layers(light.z) layers, the layer k at (u, v) + k step with step = light.xy / z * scale * (1 - h) / N and z = max(light.z, 1e-4); the light's ray height there is h + (1 - h) k / N, and the shadow is 1 - saturate(8 * max_k(max(sampled height - ray height, 0))). A light that is not above the surface (z <= 0) or a zero vector is fully shadowed.
