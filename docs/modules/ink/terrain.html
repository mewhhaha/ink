ink/terrain

3 types · 21 values

Height and normal queries for a heightfield terrain: ask for the ground at a world position, keep a body standing on it, bound it over a region.

The caller provides a grid of heights in metres: cells + 1 samples per side, row by row from the -z edge, column from the -x edge. Ground height is the bilinear grid height plus amplitude * noise.fbm2. Beyond the square the grid's border value continues (the detail does too).

The terrain is centred on origin, so the ground at the centre sits at origin.y plus the grid height there. All queries take and return world positions.

Build one Terrain per step with make and ask it as often as you like.

Key items: Terrain, Stand, Stored, make, base.

blot · runnable example
import * as math from "ink/math"
let heights = #[0.0, 2.0, 2.0, 4.0]
let ground = make math.zero3 10.0 1 (&heights[0..heights.length]) 0.0 1.0 0 0
height ground 0.0 0.0 // => 2.0

func stick 
func stick: Terrain -> Vec3 -> F32 -> F32 -> Stand

Keep a body from sinking into the ground. lift is how far its origin sits above its lowest point (a capsule's half height, a sphere's radius). A body at or below the ground is put on top of it and grounded; a body above it is left alone. Nothing here is gravity: the game moves the body first.

reach is how far above the ground a body still counts as standing on it: it is brought down onto the ground, so a character walking down a slope keeps its footing instead of hopping from step to step. A jump should pass 0 (or skip the call) on the steps where it leaves the ground.

func stand 
func stand: Terrain -> Vec3 -> F32 -> Stand

stick with no reach: only a body touching or sunk into the ground stands.

func range 
func range: Terrain -> F32 -> F32 -> F32 -> F32 -> (F32, F32)

The lowest and highest the ground can be over the world rectangle from (x0, z0) to (x1, z1) (x0 <= x1, z0 <= z1): a bound, never an underestimate, from the grid samples under the rectangle and the detail's amplitude. For broadphase tests against a body or a region. It reads every grid sample the rectangle covers, so ask about bodies, not about the whole map.
